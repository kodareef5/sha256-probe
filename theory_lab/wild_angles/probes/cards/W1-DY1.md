# W1-DY1 — Differential transfer operator + Ruelle zeta -> collisions as a leading eigenvalue   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** Build a weighted Perron-Frobenius operator L on per-round modular differentials; log2 lambda_max(L) IS 2^0.74N's exponent (0.74), N-stable; de58-low-rank says L is tiny / O(1)-rank, giving a poly-time collision counter.

**Probe run:** Built the per-round differential transfer operator L[d',d] on the (da,de) differential head with the message differential FIXED (cascade regime, msgdiff=0, an active differential injected) — the genuine "count carry realizations per round" object the card asks for. Computed its Perron eigenvalue at N=4,6,8,10 in TWO natural normalizations (probability/Markov and raw-count = the card's literal #realizations). Throttled (taskpolicy -b, OMP_NUM_THREADS=2), 30k Monte-Carlo conditioning states per head, ~16 s total. Also refit the "measured exponent" from the repo's own Figure-2 collision table.

**Result (numbers):**
- Probability operator: log2 lambda_max = 0.000 at EVERY N (4,6,8,10). A column-stochastic operator's Perron eigenvalue is identically 1 -> log2 = 0, structurally, never 0.74.
- Count operator (realizations per round = 2^N * Markov): lambda_max = 16, 64, 256, 1024 at N=4,6,8,10 -> log2 = 4.000, 6.000, 8.000, 10.000 == N exactly. Drifts with N (it is the per-round message+carry freedom), never near 0.74.
- Charitable "lambda^64 = 2^(0.74N)" back-solve: lambda would have to be 2^(0.74N/64) = 1.033, 1.049, 1.066, 1.083 — i.e. GROW with N, directly contradicting the card's own "N-stable" premise.
- The target itself is soft: refitting the repo's collision table gives pooled slope 0.673 (not 0.74) with per-(N mod 4)-class slopes spanning 0.72-1.04. "0.74" is a loose cross-class average, not a sharp constant an eigenvalue could converge onto.

**Kill_criterion:** "Dead if log2 lambda_max isn't within ~10% of the measured exponent at two N, or lambda_max drifts with N." — **fired? YES (both clauses, in both normalizations).**

**Verdict reasoning:** The kill criterion fires unambiguously. In the probability normalization log2 lambda_max = 0 at all N (off by the entire value, and N-stable at the WRONG number). In the raw-count normalization log2 lambda_max = N exactly (grows with N -> "drifts" clause fires) and is nowhere near 0.74. There is no natural normalization in between whose Perron log2 lands at 0.74. The deeper reason — and the real finding — is a units/category error baked into the card: log2 lambda_max(L) is an entropy/growth rate PER ROUND (a fixed-point property of L, N-stable by the de-law), whereas 0.74 is the growth of the terminal collision count PER UNIT WORD-WIDTH N (the slope of log2 C(N) vs N over the 4N bits of tail freedom across the whole 64-round trail). A per-round eigenvalue and a per-N slope are different quantities; their numerical agreement could only be coincidence, and here they do not even agree.

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** The card's own skeptic line ("feed-forward is terminal/non-recurrent, so 'iterate to a stationary measure' is a finite-time approximation") points at exactly this gap; the probe makes it quantitative. The de58-low-rank premise is also weaker than advertised: low |de58| is a property of the cascade-CONSTRAINED trajectory (a specific db56), but under generic conditioning the produced differential head spreads to hundreds of states (BFS hit the 400-head cap at N>=6) — so the operator is NOT O(1)-rank as a generic object. What could rescue the angle: a recurrent-interior (rounds 16-59) operator with a genuinely weighted potential whose pressure equation P(beta*)=0 yields beta* matching the rate (that is the DY2 reframing, tested separately) — but as a bare "log2 lambda_max = 0.74" identity, the angle is dead. Cross-validated independently of the eigenvalue by the dimensional argument and by the refit-exponent spread.
