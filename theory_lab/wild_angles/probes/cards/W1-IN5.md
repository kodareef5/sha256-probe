# W1-IN5 — Communication complexity of the round-60 predicate → MITM as a barrier   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** Split the collision predicate at the cut round: Alice holds forward freedoms (IV→r), Bob backward (target→r); collision ⇔ their round-r state-sets intersect = Set-Intersection/Disjointness. A communication LOWER bound would turn MITM's upper bound (232/256) into a real barrier. Probe predicts the communication matrix is **low-rank below the frontier, FULL-rank just past it** (a phase transition to high communication).

**Probe run:** Genuine N-bit reduced compression. Built the communication matrix `M[a][b]=1 iff Sf(a)==Sb(b)` on matched lanes, where Alice enumerates FA forward message words (IV→state at cut r) and Bob enumerates FB tail words and inverts rounds R−1..r from a fixed target H* (exact round inversion — validated to invert the forward round at N=4,6,8). Measured real rank, GF(2) rank, log-rank, fooling-set, and density vs cut position r, for 3 random targets. N=4 (256×256, match 2 lanes, R=22) and N=6 (64×64, match 1 lane, R=16). Throttled.

**Result (numbers):**
- **No low→full transition at any frontier — the card's prediction fails on both ends.** Diagnostic flags `shallow-low=False`, `deep-FULL=False` at both N. Shallow cuts are *mid*-rank (normrank ≈ 0.40, not low); deepest cuts are **rank ≤ 1** (not full).
- **Flat mid-rank over most cuts:** normrank ≈ 0.40 for r=3..11 (N=4), essentially constant — no rise toward a frontier.
- **A single combinatorial MITM-sweet-spot spike** at r=14 (R=22) / r=8 (R=16): normrank jumps to 0.62 / 0.66, reproducible across all 3 targets. Its position tracks **distance-to-end** (R−8), i.e. the meet-in-the-middle optimum set by FA,FB and depth — *not* an absolute round-60-style frontier.
- **Deep cuts collapse to rank-1 "always-meet"** (trivially EASY): for r past the spike, M is all-ones (rank ≤ 1) or empty — the parties' single-/double-lane sets generically intersect, i.e. LOW communication.

**Kill_criterion:** "Dead if the matrix is LOW-rank on BOTH sides (always easy) OR FULL-rank EVERYWHERE (no transition)." — **fired? yes** (deep cuts are rank≤1 = low-rank/always-easy exactly where the card needs a high-communication barrier; and the predicted low→full transition is absent).

**Verdict reasoning:** The card's load-bearing structure — low-rank below the frontier, *full-rank (high-communication barrier) just past it* — is contradicted at small N for every target tested. The observed rank profile is the signature of MITM as an **upper bound**: a meet-in-the-middle sweet-spot (one rank spike at the combinatorially optimal cut) flanked by easy regimes, with the **deepest** cuts being rank-1 "always intersect" (trivially easy) rather than a barrier. There is no emergent Disjointness/high-rank pattern at the deep (near-frontier) cuts. So the reframe of MITM from upper bound to communication lower bound does not materialize — KILLED, as stated (the deep, near-frontier predicate is low-rank/always-easy, kill clause A's spirit, and no transition to "full just past" occurs).

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** A clean negative; not softened. The result is stable across N=4 and N=6 and three independent random targets. Two honest caveats, neither of which rescues the card: (1) the *literal* full-state MITM predicate (match all 8 lanes) gives an **empty** matrix at this small freedom (the well-known MITM rarity), so I matched 1–2 lanes to get a structured matrix; the rank-1 deep-cut collapse is partly because a single mixed lane intersects generically — but that *is* the "low communication = easy" outcome, the opposite of a barrier, so it cuts against the card either way. (2) Reachable R (≤22) cannot place the cut at the *relative* depth of round 60/64 ≈ 94%; the spike I see tracks distance-to-end, not an intrinsic round, and there is no sign that increasing R would manufacture a high-rank deep-cut barrier (deep cuts get *more* trivial, not less). The card's own skeptic is vindicated: "explicit communication lower bounds are a graveyard… expect to *measure* rank, not *prove* a bound" — measured, the rank says MITM-upper-bound, not lower-bound-barrier. A genuinely decisive probe would need the full-state predicate with large freedom (many words) at a relative cut depth near 0.94 — far beyond this cheap small-N reach (would be REBUILD-NEEDED), but the small-N evidence already points away from the barrier.
