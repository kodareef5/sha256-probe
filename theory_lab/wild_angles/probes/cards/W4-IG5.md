# W4-IG5 — Jeffreys volume: 0.74 as the Fisher–Rao volume-growth exponent   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** the count of statistically-distinguishable near-collision input directions = the Fisher (Jeffreys) volume ∫√det(g); with effective metric-dimension d_eff<N (the IG1 degeneracy) it grows as 2^{cN} with c≈0.74 — tying 0.74 and 132 to the same metric.

**Probe run:** two independent estimates of the growth exponent. (A) Collision-count slope: fit log₂C(N) over the canonical MSB-kernel sr=60 counts {4:49, 8:260, 10:946, 12:2955} (260@N=8, 946@N=10 reproduced fresh this run), with all adjacent-pair slopes to expose the spread. (B) Direct Fisher–Rao volume: build the ε-Bernoulli pullback metric g=JᵀJ (Bernoulli weight) on 4N input directions × 256 output bits at N=8,10,12, take log₂√det = ½Σlog₂λ via Jacobi eigenvalues, fit its slope, and test sampling sensitivity. Throttled (OMP=2, taskpolicy -b). Reuses lib.sha256.

**Result (numbers):**
- **[A]** global least-squares slope = **0.740** (matches the repo's 4-pt fit) — BUT adjacent-pair slopes are **0.602, 0.932, 0.822** (range [0.602, 0.932], band **0.330**). |0.74−0.673| = 0.067 is **~5× smaller than the 0.330 pair-spread** → 0.74 and 0.673 are **statistically indistinguishable** at this data resolution.
- **[B]** Fisher rank is **FULL** at every N (32/32, 40/40, 48/48) → **Fisher-flat dim = 0** (confirms IG1; the card's d_eff<N degeneracy is absent). The direct √det volume slope = **8.36 per N** (re-seed **8.19**) — *not* 0.74, and sampling-sensitive. (√det of a full-rank 4N-dim metric grows ≈ 4N·O(1), an order of magnitude off the claimed 2^{0.74N}.)

**Kill_criterion:** "slope clearly ≠ 0.74, or wildly sensitive to ε/weighting (no intrinsic exponent)." — **fired? YES, on both clauses.** The collision-count 0.74 is real *as a fit* but not *sharp* (indistinguishable from 0.673 within the 0.33 pair-spread); and the direct Fisher–Rao volume slope is 8.36 (not 0.74) and varies with sampling (8.36→8.19).

**Verdict reasoning:** KILLED, exactly per prior-finding #2. The famous 0.740 is the global slope of a 4-point collision-count fit whose per-segment slopes scatter over 0.60–0.93 — a spread that swallows the 0.067 difference from 0.673, so the card's "specific 0.74" is not a sharp, intrinsic exponent. Worse, the card's *mechanism* — a degenerate Fisher metric (d_eff<N) whose surviving √det-growth "sets 0.74" — is contradicted by direct measurement: the Fisher metric is **full-rank** (Fisher-flat dim 0, matching IG1), and its actual √det volume grows at ≈8.4/N, not 0.74/N. So 0.74 is neither sharp as a number nor produced by the proposed Fisher–Rao volume; it is a collision-*count* fit artifact, conflated with a metric-volume integral that gives a completely different number.

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** The card's own skeptic ("deriving a specific 0.74 from a volume integral is the most numerology-prone ask; √det is noisy and worst-estimated on small eigenvalues; treat a hit as suggestive only unless robust across N/ε and non-circular with IG1") is fully vindicated: there is no small-eigenvalue degeneracy at all (full rank), the volume slope is the wrong magnitude (8.4) and sampling-sensitive, and the only place 0.74 appears (collision counts) is non-sharp. Independent corroboration: IG1/W2-CT1 (Fisher corank 0, full rank), and the wave preamble's conditional "if IG1's corank isn't ≈132, IG5 falls" — it does. A skeptic's second look was warranted on the suspicious global-slope match to 0.740; it dissolves under the pair-slope spread and the contradicting direct-volume number.
