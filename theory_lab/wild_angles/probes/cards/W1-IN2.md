# W1-IN2 — XOR/ADD uncertainty principle → a provable "no basis is easy" barrier   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** For a SHA round R, `support_⊕(R) · support_+(R) ≥ 2^{cn}` — any function sparse in the Walsh (XOR) basis is forced dense in the cyclic-DFT (ADD) basis, so "no basis diagonalizes both." Sold as a *provable support-product lower bound with a constant c* that retires ANF/linear/2-adic at once.

**Probe run:** Built the genuine N-bit SHA round (`transfer_operator._make_round`, scaled rotations). Froze a random interior state + round constant, let one schedule word `w` vary over all 2^N values, read the e-output `e' = d + T1 mod 2^N`, took each output bit → a Boolean function f(w). Computed Walsh-Hadamard spectrum (S_⊕) and length-2^N cyclic FFT spectrum (S_+). Measured `log2(S_⊕·S_+)` vs N under **two** support definitions — entropy/effective support (the card's mandated robust one) and hard support — for N=4..16 (8 then 4 random states per N, all bits). Control: same round with `+`→`^`. Throttled.

**Result (numbers):**
- **Effective (entropy) support product SATURATES / shrinks.** `log2(S_⊕·S_+)` over N=8,10,12,14,16 = 3.21, 3.83, 4.02, 3.66, 4.08 — **flat, oscillating, no trend**. Per-N normalization `prod/N` falls monotonically 0.57→0.51→0.45→0.39→0.32→0.26→0.255.
- **The "constant" is a fit artifact.** Naive no-intercept c = mean(log2 prod)/mean(N) = **0.30** *only because* a saturating constant (~3.6) is amortized over N; as larger N is added the **with-intercept slope collapses 0.19 → 0.078** and the intercept rises to 2.8. Marginal slopes d(log2 prod)/dN oscillate around zero: +0.31, +0.09, **−0.18**, +0.21. The spectrum gets *more* concentrated with N (at N=16, 90% of Walsh mass is in a **single** atom; effective Walsh support = 1.52).
- **Hard support product = exactly 2^N** (8.0, 12.0, 16.0 at N=8,12,16 → c=1.0 clean) — but this is the **trivial Donoho–Stark floor** `|supp_W|·|supp_C| ≥ |G| = 2^N` that holds for *every* non-degenerate function on Z/2^N (a random function too), not a SHA barrier; and it is driven entirely by the **tiny coefficients the card explicitly told us to exclude** (at N=16, hard count 256 vs 1 coefficient holding 90% of the mass).
- **Control collapses as predicted** (good sanity check): XOR-round product slope ≈ 0.02–0.06 ≈ 0, effective Walsh support →1.

**Kill_criterion:** "Dead if log₂(S_⊕·S_+) is flat/sub-linear in N, or a cheap basis change drives it below 2^{0.3N}." — **fired? yes**

**Verdict reasoning:** Under the *effective/entropy* support the card itself mandates ("use a robust effective support"), `log2(S_⊕·S_+)` is not just sub-linear — it is **flat and even decreasing** in N (kill clause A fires). The only way to manufacture linear growth is the *hard* support, which (i) counts exactly the negligible coefficients the card warned against, and (ii) gives merely the universal group-theoretic floor `≥ 2^N` (c=1 for *all* functions, including random — it is not a property of SHA's mixing). So there is no SHA-specific, robust, super-`2^{0.3N}` constant: either the bound is the trivial Donoho–Stark identity, or, robustly measured, it collapses. KILLED as stated.

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** A surprising-looking SURVIVES (the 0.30 no-intercept number) was the trap — chased to N=16 it evaporates, so I am NOT softening to SURVIVES. Three independent angles agree: (1) effective-support product is flat/shrinking; (2) the only linear law is the universal `2^N` floor, basis-independent of SHA; (3) the closed form of f as a function of w is literally `bit_b(w + const)`, a *single modular shift* — its spectra are exactly the textbook XOR-vs-cyclic concentration of a shift, with no SHA-specific amplification, so there is no reason to expect a SHA constant > the universal floor. Could a 64-round composite (vs one lane-bit) show genuine joint density? Possibly, but the card's stated cheapest probe is the lane bit, and it kills the simple form; any rescue requires the *composite* product to exceed `2^N` super-linearly, which this single-round object gives no evidence for (it sits exactly at the universal floor). The Wave-1 lesson applies in reverse here: the *hard*-support "linearity" is the harmonic-analysis analogue of the XOR-linearization artifact — a uniform, content-free `2^N` that isn't the real obstruction.
