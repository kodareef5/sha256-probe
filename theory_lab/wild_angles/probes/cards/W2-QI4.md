# W2-QI4 — Stabilizer rank χ → branch count of any case-splitting search saturates at the wall   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** The minimum affine-piece cover of each round (its stabilizer rank χ) counts carry case-split branches; **Σ log₂ χ_i explodes near round 59**. Probe (N=2..8): per round build the truth table, compute the minimum affine cover χ_i (greedy set-cover over affine subfunctions); track Σ log₂ χ_i; measure χ of the *cumulative* map (where ranks multiply); does it grow **super-linearly toward the budget near 59**? cross-plot vs QI1's μ(i).

**Probe run:** N=2,3,4, throttled. χ = greedy affine-piece cover count: scan the domain, a point joins an existing piece iff the piece stays GF(2)-affine-consistent on all its points (tested by Gaussian elimination of the augmented system per output coordinate), else opens a new piece. (A) χ of the ISOLATED round components (carry of modular add, Ch, Maj) at width N. (B) χ of the CUMULATIVE mini-SHA map `x→state_after_round_r` over a small input subspace (in_dim≤10, domain 64/256), advanced incrementally round-by-round; tracked χ(r) and Σ log₂ χ(r) over 24 rounds.

**Result (numbers):**
- (A) Isolated per-round χ **grows with N** (not strictly O(1)): add-carry 2,5,9; Ch 5,13,42; Maj 4,13,37 at N=2,3,4 — roughly exponential in N, but the round function is identical each round so this is a *constant per round*.
- (B) The CUMULATIVE-map χ(r) **saturates at round 3** and is flat for all remaining rounds:
  - N=3: χ(r) = 7,8,**10**,10,10,… (max 10 of domain 64; reaches max at round 3, flat after).
  - N=4: χ(r) = 16,27,**29**,29,29,… (max 29 of domain 256; reaches max at round 3, flat after).
- Consequently **Σ log₂ χ(r) is a perfectly straight line** — constant slope once χ saturates (N=4: 4.0, 8.75, 13.61, 18.47, … adding ≈4.86 every round). **No super-linear growth, no explosion, no knee near round 59** — Σlog₂χ at round 24 is just 24×(constant).

**Kill_criterion:** "Dead if χ_i is O(1) and round-independent even for the cumulative map (merely linear growth, no saturation)." — **fired? YES** (operative clause: cumulative χ is round-independent — saturates at round 3 — and Σlog₂χ is *merely linear*, with no super-linear approach to any budget near 59).

**Verdict reasoning:** KILLED on the card's actual claim. The entire content is that "Σ log₂ χ_i explodes near round 59" and that two independent magic measures (this and QI1) land on ~59. Both are false: the cumulative-map χ saturates by **round 3** (≪59), so every subsequent round multiplies the branch count by the *same constant factor*, making Σ log₂ χ grow strictly **linearly** with no super-linear blow-up and no knee anywhere — least of all at 59. It cannot mark a boundary 55+ rounds after it stops changing. This converges with W2-QI1 (contextual magic saturates at round 4) and lead finding #4 (no round-60 knee; everything saturates early and smoothly). The "timeout onset = round where Σlog₂χ exceeds budget" story has no inflection to onset at — the curve is a ramp from round 3.

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** The one literal nuance: χ_i is *not* strictly O(1) — it grows ~exponentially in word width N (Ch/Maj at N=4 already need ~40 affine pieces). So a purist could say the first kill clause ("χ_i is O(1)") doesn't fire verbatim. But that does not save the card: a per-round χ that is *constant across rounds* (which it is — identical round function, cumulative χ flat after round 3) yields exactly "merely linear growth, no saturation [toward the wall]", which is the kill's decisive clause, and it kills the headline "explodes near round 59" outright. (If anything, χ_i growing with N but not with *round* makes the round-59 explosion even more impossible.) Caveats: greedy affine cover over-counts χ vs the true minimum (NP-hard), and the cumulative-map χ is measured on a low-dim input subspace, but both biases would only inflate later-round χ — yet it still flatlines at round 3, so the conclusion is robust. The branch-count-vs-round picture provides no second magic measure landing on 59; like QI1, it saturates immediately.
