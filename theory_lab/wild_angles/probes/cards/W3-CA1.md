**Card claim:** The cascade is a state-based very-well-behaved lens (get = recover M1-view; put = correct M2 = casoff). Its three laws map as GetPut→vacuous (da=0 held), PutGet→`g1=0` per-message value match, PutPut→`h=0` difference compatibility — and `2^-2N` = "drop PutGet lose 2^-N, drop PutPut lose another 2^-N" on **distinct** laws.

**Probe run:** Per-round violation rates of the 3 laws at the boundary round W[60] over the FULL de61=0 hit population (repo `gap_analysis.c`, rebuilt lab-side at N=8 → 16.2M hits), plus the exact rank-2 / PutPut identity `g2 = g1 + h (mod 2^N)` over the 946-collision CSV (N=10). N=10 full-population numbers cited from the repo's VERIFIED 1B-sample run (timed out live under the 2-thread courtesy budget — 2^30 triples). Throttled (OMP=2, taskpolicy -b).

**Result (numbers):**

- **GetPut vacuity:** the casoff correction holds da=0 on **946/946** of the population (the g2=g1+h closure is exactly the da=0 cascade certificate) ⇒ GetPut violation rate **0.0000** at r≤60, as the card requires.

**Kill_criterion:** "if the rates don't partition cleanly, OR both g1,h map to the *same* law (can't explain 2^-2N vs 2^-N), OR GetPut shows violations at r≤60." — **fired? no.** Partition is clean; g1,h are provably distinct laws; GetPut rate is 0.

**Verdict reasoning:** SURVIVES, **not** CONFIRMED — and the call is the rename-vs-mechanism one (finding #4). The lens dictionary {GetPut↔da=0, PutGet↔g1, PutPut↔h} fits the data perfectly, but g1 and h are *already* CONFIRMED reals (PH1/IN3/NT4), so reproducing the clean partition is necessary, not sufficient, for CONFIRMED. The probe asked specifically whether the lens-LAW structure predicts/computes anything g1,h alone do not. The only candidate new content is the **closure argument** (M1+M2): a state-based lens has *exactly* {GetPut, PutGet, PutPut}, GetPut is forced vacuous by the held da=0 invariant, so exactly **two** well-behavedness laws remain — i.e. "why exactly two, not three" (a 2^-3N boundary would need a third independent obstruction, which is absent). That is a real *structural* story for the integer 2, and it is consistent with the data — but it does **not** independently DERIVE the 2 (it assumes the cascade is a well-behaved lens; it relabels the absence of a third condition as "no fourth law") and it predicts no new number, no the 1.005 ratio is an *input* to declaring the lens "very-well-behaved," not an output. So: faithful rename + a suggestive-but-non-load-bearing closure argument. Promote only if someone can turn closure into a proof that no third boundary condition can exist (which would upgrade "why 2^-2N not 2^-3N" to a theorem).

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** The honest risk the card itself flags ("clean *renaming* of g1→PutGet") is realized. Guards: I verified (a) the two laws are algebraically distinct (h ⊥ g1, 240/627), (b) GetPut is genuinely 0 (not assumed), (c) the product law holds at two widths (0.923 @N=8, repo 1.005 @N=10) — so it is at least a *correct* rename, with the partition falsifiable and unfired. What would make it a mechanism: a lens-theoretic prediction the raw g1/h analysis cannot make — e.g. deriving the per-step independence (1.005) from a PutGet⊥PutPut theorem, or proving completeness so 2^-3N is impossible. Absent that, CONFIRMED would overclaim. Skeptic caveat: "very-well-behaved lens" is a *post-hoc* label chosen because the laws happen to split this way; the framing earns its keep only if the closure claim is proved, not assumed.
