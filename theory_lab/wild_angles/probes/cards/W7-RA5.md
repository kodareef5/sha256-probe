# W7-RA5 — Ramsey-number clique threshold for the boundary round   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** The message-word agreement graph (edge RED iff the pair stays a partial collision through r); a multi-word collision family = a RED clique; the boundary r* = where the coloring first forces/loses the needed RED clique (a Ramsey-number-vs-edge-density condition).

**Probe run:** N=4,8,10, K=32 and 64 sampled tail free-word vectors. Built the agreement graph via the cascade engine (`_w5co_engine` + `_hc_family.tail_trace`): per-vertex modular diff trace rounds 57..63; RED-through-r = both vertices are full 8-register partial collisions through r; tracked also the de=0 (register-e alive) survivor count. Exact max clique (Bron–Kerbosch). Plus two follow-ups (`_ra5_scale.py`): the Ramsey forcing-scale vs SHA dimensions, and K-independence of the boundary collapse (K=16..1024). Throttled.

**Result (numbers):** Full-8-register RED clique = 1 (trivial) at EVERY round for random vertices (random tails essentially never fully collide) → max clique = the random Erdős value, no boundary anomaly. The de=0 survivor count shows the established picture: round 60 = K survivors (fraction 1.000 at all K=16..1024) = the FREE cascade plateau; round 61 collapses to fraction ~0.07 (N=4) / ~0.000–0.006 (N=8 ≈ 2^−N), and this fraction is CONSTANT across K (no onset at a special K*). Scale: forcing a size-s RED clique needs K~2^(s/2); SHA's needed clique is s≤4 (one free cascade axis) → K~4 (trivially met); a clique comparable to 64 rounds would need K~2^32, dwarfing SHA.

**Kill_criterion:** "max clique = the random Erdős value with no anomaly at the boundary, OR no drop at sr+1." — **fired? yes (both prongs).** (a) The full-collision RED clique IS the trivial random value (1) with no anomaly. (b) The one drop (round 61) is the schedule condition — K-independent, not a Ramsey-vs-vertex-count forcing.

**Verdict reasoning:** A Ramsey threshold forces a size-s monochromatic clique once the *vertex count* K crosses R(s); here the clique structure is fixed by the SHA round function (de=0 or not), independent of K — the survivor fraction at round 61 is flat across K=16→1024, so there is no Ramsey-in-K onset. The boundary IS real (the well-known sr=60/61 wall: free cascade through 60, schedule condition at 61, with N=8 collapse ≈2^−N), but it is a round-function fact, not a clique-forcing-by-vertex-count phenomenon. Per finding #4 the Ramsey scale dwarfs 64 rounds (the cliques SHA needs are size ≤4, forced at K~4; matching 64 rounds needs K~2^32) → non-match. This is the card's own "weakest of the five," and only a sharp clique collapse *driven by a Ramsey number* would redeem it — none exists.

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** What would overturn KILLED: a RED-clique onset at a specific vertex count K* tracking R(s,t) for the actual needed s. Tested K=16,64,256,1024 — fraction flat, no onset. Independent corroboration: round-60 fraction is exactly 1.000 (whole-cube free cascade, finding #4: no round-60 knee) and the only feature is the round-61 schedule condition, whose rate (~2^−N at N=8) matches the established per-round gating, not any Ramsey number.
