**Probe run:** N=8 mini-SHA, rotations scaled exactly as the repo C tools (self-validated: 260/260 actual collisions replayed and verified as full collisions). P_bwd(r) = de_r=(e1−e2) law over the 260 real sr=60 collisions; P_fwd(r) = de_r over a 20 000-sample forward sweep of free (w57,w58,w59,w60). KL(P_bwd‖P_fwd) per round r=57..63, plus two skeptic tests: (A) is the jump intrinsic or the cascade control-horizon entropy? (B) does Pythagoras hold/break across the boundary? **Kill_criterion:** "KL increases smoothly (no jump), or Pythagoras holds/fails equally on both sides." — **fired? YES, on both clauses.** Inside the named de57..de60 window KL is flat (max increment 0.15); the only large increment is at the cascade's free-variable horizon (60→61), and its size is *exactly* the control-horizon entropy log₂2^N — an artifact of where my free variables stop, not an emergent geometric defect. And Pythagoras holds (it does not hold-then-break across the boundary).

**Verdict reasoning:** KILLED. The apparent 8-bit "jump" is not a dual-flatness rupture but the trivial consequence of the cascade construction controlling rounds 57–60 and leaving 61–63 unconstrained: P_fwd is a near-point-mass while da is forced (r≤60) and becomes uniform the instant forcing ends (r≥61), so KL against the collapsed backward law jumps by exactly the gained entropy (log₂2^N=8). Shift the free-variable window and the "jump" would move with it — it tracks the control horizon, not round 61 intrinsically. This matches prior-finding #4 (no special round-60 knee; structural quantities saturate smoothly). The Pythagorean structure does not distinguish the two sides. The card itself flagged this as "the weakest of the five," and the data confirm it.
