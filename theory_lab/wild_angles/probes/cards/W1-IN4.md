# W1-IN4 — 2D PEPS over the round×bit lattice → an entanglement anisotropy   ·   VERDICT: KILLED

**Card claim:** The killed MPS cut 1D along bits (carry chain → bond dim ~2^N → death). A 2D round×bit tensor network might be **area-law across the ROUND-cut** (= MITM cut, only ~24/256 bits cross) even where the bit-cut is volume-law. Predict bit-cut ≈ 2^N but round-cut grows SLOWLY (area law) with a knee near the frontier — an anisotropy.

**Probe run:** Genuine N-bit reduced compression (`transfer_operator._make_round`). Did NOT contract; measured **Rényi-0 boundary rank = log2(# distinct boundary configs)** for both cuts. ROUND-cut at position r = log2(# distinct full 8-lane states after r rounds) over all messages. BIT-cut at position β = log2(# distinct per-round carry-straddling slices of the adder lanes a,e). Free message words F=2,3 (words feed rounds 0..F-1, so after r rounds min(r,F)·N bits are injected → an exact **area-law defect** test: DEFECT = injected − log2(#distinct)). N=4,6, R up to 20. Throttled.

**Result (numbers):**
- **Round-cut boundary rank tracks injected freedom with ZERO defect.** N=4,F=3: log2#states after r=0,1,2,3 = 0, 4, 8, 12 = injected exactly; **DEFECT = +0.000 at every round**. N=6,F=3: 0, 6, 12, 18 = injected; **DEFECT = +0.000 everywhere**. The round map is injective on the reachable set (full-rank, volume-law) — distinct trajectories *never merge*.
- **Round-cut slope is MAXIMAL:** N bits/round until the freedom is exhausted (slope/N = **1.0**, ≥ 0.8). Saturates at the input-freedom cap by round F (e.g. round 2 for F=2). **No knee, no area law, no compression.**
- **Bit-cut** also saturates immediately at the same cap (log2 ≈ F·N by β=1). So round-cut and bit-cut are *equally* volume-law — there is no anisotropy; the round-cut is not slower than the bit-cut.

**Kill_criterion:** "Dead if round-cut boundary rank grows AS FAST AS the bit-cut (slope ≥ 0.8/round) — brute force in every direction." — **fired? yes**

**Verdict reasoning:** The round-cut boundary rank grows at the *maximum possible* rate (slope/N = 1.0, defect identically 0), i.e. exactly as fast as — not slower than — the bit-cut. There is no area law and no knee near any frontier: every distinct input maps to a distinct intermediate state (the compression round is a state-permutation for fixed message, so reachability is injective). The hoped-for anisotropy (slow round-cut, fast bit-cut) does not exist at small N — both directions are volume-law. KILLED, as stated; brute force in every direction.

**Cross-check / skeptic note:** A clean negative; not softened. The zero-defect result is the strongest possible form of the kill (not merely "slope ≥ 0.8" but slope = full input rate with *no* rank loss). It is precisely the Wave-1 spine made concrete: the round Jacobian is uniformly full-rank/expanding, so no cut sees an area law. **Honest caveat on the card's MITM number:** the "~24/256 bits cross" figure refers to the *backward* cut with a FIXED target hash constraining the state — a different object than the forward reachability boundary the card's stated probe measures. A genuinely 2-sided PEPS contraction (forward freedom meeting a fixed backward target at round r) could in principle show a narrower waist at the meet-in-the-middle round; this probe does not build that bidirectional object. But the card explicitly stakes the *forward* "round-cut grows slowly (area law) with a knee," and that is decisively false (defect ≡ 0). Reduced→full-round extrapolation risk cuts the *wrong* way here: full-rankness is if anything more complete at 32-bit width. To resurrect any anisotropy one would need the 2-sided cut to show sub-volume boundary rank, which is a different (and much harder) probe than the one the card names.
